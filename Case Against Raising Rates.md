# Case Against Raising Interest Rates

## Executive Summary
Based on current economic indicators and market conditions, the Federal Reserve should hold rates steady rather than hike. Inflation concerns are overstated due to measurement distortions, real economic fundamentals remain sound, and rate increases would not achieve their intended outcomes.

---

## 1. Inflation is Not as High as It Appears

### The CPI vs PCE Discrepancy
- **Core CPI for August**: Expected ~2.3% year-over-year—essentially in line with the Fed's 2% target
- **Core PCE**: Running at 3.3%, appearing elevated
- **The Gap**: 100 basis points between the two is driven by only **two items**, not broad-based inflation:
  1. **Financial services fees** (~60% of the gap) - reflects portfolio management fees and advisors, not experienced by most Americans
  2. **Flash memory prices** (major component of remainder) - up 9 standard deviations but irrelevant to average consumer behavior

### Why This Matters
- The average American doesn't perceive inflation from stock market gains or flash memory prices
- Core PCE's elevation is a **measurement error**, not true consumer inflation
- Core CPI is the more accurate reflection of real inflation facing households

### Supporting Evidence
- Fed Governor Chris Waller acknowledged this distinction in recent Reuters conversation
- The measurement error means hiking rates based on PCE would address a phantom problem
- Current policies are still disinflationary according to Waller

---

## 2. Corporate Credit Markets Show No Distress

### High Yield Spreads: The Canary in the Coal Mine
- High yield credit spreads are behaving **normally**—not signaling corporate distress
- This market is the single best indicator of economic trouble and deteriorating credit quality
- Unlike the yield curve (which poorly predicts recessions), high yield OAS (options-adjusted spreads) is highly reliable
- **No deterioration in corporate credit quality is being priced in**

### Implication
- If the economy were heading for trouble, the market most sensitive to that risk would be showing warning signs
- It's not. This is a contrarian positive signal.

---

## 3. Rate Hikes Won't Solve the Real Problem: AI Infrastructure Build

### The Ineffectiveness of Monetary Tightening
- AI hyperscalers justify their infrastructure spending based on expected returns, not fed funds rates
- These companies could justify their capex even if the Fed raised rates **100 basis points**
- Raising rates won't slow down this spending—the underlying economics are too compelling

### The Infrastructure Boom is Healthy
- We're in a $30 trillion infrastructure build phase globally
- This represents the transition from the post-GFC underinvestment era
- Private investment as percentage of GDP was below depreciation for more than a decade after 2008
- We finally have capex coming back—this shouldn't be suppressed with rate hikes

### Long-term Inflation Isn't From Current Spending
- The infrastructure is being financed by debt markets
- But this is addressing an actual supply constraint (decades of underinvestment)
- This is not demand-pull inflation—it's supply-side investment

---

## 4. Labor Market Realities Support Rate Holds

### Global Labor Scarcity
- Labor growth is slow globally—not just in the US
- AI is addressing a genuine labor shortage problem
- Raising rates would worsen the labor shortage problem, not improve it
- We need productive capacity (capital + AI), not tighter monetary conditions

### Employment Data
- August jobs report came in stronger than expected
- This removes the case for "cooling" the labor market with rate hikes
- High yield credit spreads show employers remain confident

---

## 5. Consumer Inflation Expectations Remain Anchored

### Survey Evidence
- Consumer surveys do NOT show expectations of higher inflation
- **NY Fed survey of inflation expectations**: Down 3 months in a row
- **NFIB survey**: Inflation as a business problem trending down (spiked in 2022 and early 2026 from Iran war, but declining)
- **Conference Board and Umish surveys**: All corroborating downtrend in inflation expectations

### Why This Matters
- If consumers don't expect higher inflation, there's no wage-price spiral risk
- The Fed's job is easier when inflation expectations are anchored
- This argues for patience, not preemptive hikes

---

## 6. Energy Costs Are Not Burdensome Despite Recent Spikes

### Gasoline Burden on Household Budgets
- **Current gasoline burden**: 2.2% of household spending
- **65-year historical average**: ~3%
- **GFC era (high levels)**: 4.5%
- **Oil embargo (peak burden)**: 6-12% of household wallets

### Key Point
- Even after the recent Iran war-related spike in oil prices, energy costs remain well below both historical averages and prior crisis levels
- There's no demand-driven inflation pressure from energy consuming households

---

## 7. Growth is Broadening, Not Narrowing

### Positive Fundamentals
- Good earnings visibility backed by AI
- ISM recovery showing manufacturing strength
- Broadening expansion across sectors (not concentrated)
- Small cap outperformance indicates breadth

### The Real Economic Story
- We're not seeing unsustainable boom dynamics
- We're seeing a rebalancing from underinvestment (post-GFC) to appropriate capex levels
- This is healthy, not inflationary

---

## 8. Market Evidence Supports Holding Rates

### Recent Market Signals
- Best performing assets in Q3: Crypto, Energy, Tech (downstream AI plays)
- 4 of top 21 Russell 1000 performers are crypto stocks
- This performance breadth suggests confidence in a holding scenario
- Prediction markets and Fed futures still showing ~60% probability of hike, but this seems to price old concerns

---

## 9. The "Economists are Fighting Last Year's Wars" Problem

### Historical Pattern
- Post-GFC, consensus economists predicted slow growth ("new normal")
- This depressed business investment for over a decade
- Similar dynamic happening now with inflation fears
- Current data doesn't support the inflation narrative that consensus economists are using

### The Solution
- Fed should demonstrate patience and flexibility
- Allow data to accumulate showing disinflationary trends
- By end of 2026, demonstrable inflation progress will likely be evident

---

## 10. Political and Geopolitical Context

### Complicating Factors
- Iran war creating periodic commodity spikes
- Midterm elections creating uncertainty
- These are temporary, not structural inflation drivers

### Fed Should Stay Above the Fray
- Monetary policy shouldn't be hostage to geopolitical volatility
- The data on underlying inflation is clear: it's not a problem worth hiking over

---

## Conclusion

**The case for a rate hold is strong:**
1. Real inflation (Core CPI) is near target when measurement errors are excluded
2. Corporate credit markets show no distress
3. Rate hikes won't solve the AI spending issue
4. Labor market is healthy but faces structural constraints
5. Consumer inflation expectations remain anchored
6. Energy costs are not burdensome
7. Growth fundamentals are solid and broadening
8. Markets are pricing a hold scenario
9. Economists are overreacting to past patterns
10. Current policy remains disinflationary

**The Fed should maintain its current rate structure and demonstrate patience. The risks of premature hiking outweigh any benefits.**
