The Fed Has Reached Maximum Pain—Reversal Coming

Analysis by Tom Lee | Fundstrat | September 25, 2026

Kevin Worsh's recent surprise Fed hike contradicts market signals. The stock market's face-ripper rally following the hike and crypto's parabolic move aren't signs of monetary tightness working—they're signals that the Fed has already reached maximum pain and will reverse course.

The Contradiction in Market Reaction

A Fed hike should tighten financial conditions. If the hike was warranted—if the economy was overheating or inflation was genuinely problematic—stocks should fall. Instead, stocks rallied. Crypto went parabolic.

The Signal: If a Fed hike triggers a stock market face-ripper and crypto surge, the hike is a policy error, not a calibration. Markets are telling us the Fed went too far.

This isn't a market misunderstanding monetary policy. This is the market correctly diagnosing that the Fed has reached the point where additional tightness is counterproductive and unsustainable.

Maximum Pain: When Policy Becomes Unaffordable

The Three Clues

Maximum pain describes the moment when policy rates become unaffordable for the real economy—when businesses, governments, and consumers can no longer service debt at current rates without distress.

  • Credit markets reprice: Oracle's force majeure on Project Jupiter debt signals companies can't fund operations at current rates
  • Market rallies on rate hikes: Counterintuitive, but signals the market believes the Fed will cut
  • Equities and crypto align: Risk assets surge in unison, indicating relief that tightening cycle is over

One month before Worsh's hike, the Fed was signaling rate cuts. Within one month, they pivoted to hiking. This whipsaw—combined with market euphoria following the hike—indicates the Fed overshot.

The Fed Will Move Back to Neutral, Then Dovish

Our base case has been consistent: inflation is set to fall sharply. The Fed's recent hike was a tactical error that the market is pricing as already reversible.

Our Forecast: The Fed moves from maximum pain toward neutral policy, then toward dovish accommodation. This is bullish for equities and crypto.

Understanding the Inflation Narrative

Core CPI vs. Core PCE: The Critical Difference

Markets are misled by inflation headlines. The story is more complex than consensus understands.

  • Core CPI: Running at 2.35%, essentially at the Fed's 2% target
  • Core PCE: Running at 3.4%, 1.1 percentage points higher than CPI

Why the discrepancy? The PCE measures financial services as a pure "price change" without adjusting for quantity changes. Here's the distortion:

"Every time the stock market goes up, asset management fees go up. That automatically adds to core PCE inflation. This is a huge component of the 1.1 point gap between PCE and CPI."

When asset values rise, financial services charges rise (fees are usually percentage-based). The PCE methodology counts this as inflation. It's not inflation; it's the interaction between rising asset values and fee-based pricing structures.

What This Means

True underlying inflation is lower than the Fed's preferred PCE metric suggests. This gives the Fed cover to cut rates without admitting it miscalculated on the hike.

As equities and crypto stabilize (or decline from recent highs), asset management fees moderate, and core PCE compression accelerates. The Fed's recent hike will be portrayed as a minor policy adjustment, not a major tightening cycle.

Bullish for Equities and Crypto

The sequence is already playing out: hike, market rallies, Fed reverses, rates fall. This is the most bullish scenario for risk assets.

  • Equities have room to run in a transitioning-from-max-pain environment
  • Crypto breaks out because the Fed pivot makes digital assets the preferred hedge against currency debasement
  • Credit spreads should tighten as market confidence returns
  • Earnings power increases as businesses refinance debt at lower rates
The Setup: Fed at maximum pain, hike is a mistake, equities/crypto rally in anticipation of reversal, inflation proves transitory, policy normalizes toward dovish. This is how the 2026 bull market plays out.

What to Watch

  • Fed communications in coming weeks—will Worsh backtrack?
  • Core PCE declining toward 2-2.5% as the stock market stabilizes
  • Credit spreads: if they widen further, Fed will be forced to cut faster
  • Crypto strength: sustained strength indicates market confidence in Fed reversal

The contrarian position: the Fed hiked into weakness, the market is pricing the reversal, and the next 6-12 months will see a pivot to dovish policy. Risk assets are positioned for gains as this narrative unfolds.
