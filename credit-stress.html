Credit Stress Signals: The Return of Too-Big-To-Fail

Analysis by Steve Eisman | Contrarian Finance | September 27, 2026

The financial system is showing warning signs reminiscent of 2008. Oracle's invocation of force majeure on Project Jupiter debt, Blue Owl Capital's asset downgrades, and widespread off-balance sheet financing through special purpose vehicles suggest leverage and credit stress are building beneath the surface of bullish sentiment.

The Oracle-Blue Owl Warning

Oracle's notice to Blue Owl Capital invoking force majeure on the Project Jupiter data center represents a critical warning signal. The company is attempting to defer payments on a project experiencing delays—a tactic typically deployed when cash flow pressures mount.

Project Jupiter Debt: Trading at 90 cents on the dollar, reflecting stress levels that indicate market recognition of credit risk

Oracle's situation mirrors the pattern of corporate stress: weak cash flows despite headline growth, high debt levels, and credit ratings under pressure. This isn't cyclical weakness. It's structural deterioration masked by AI enthusiasm and equity market momentum.

The Leverage Problem

Off-Balance Sheet Financing Returns

What's most disturbing is the resurgence of off-balance sheet financing through special purpose vehicles (SPVs)—the same financial engineering that characterized the 2008 crisis.

  • Debt is being kept off balance sheets through SPV structures
  • Companies like Meta are using SoftBank junk financing for equity investments
  • "Circular financing" structures in AI data center deals
  • Debt-funded AI capex being concealed from traditional balance sheet analysis
"Some aspects of AI circular financing are particularly disturbing. We're seeing the same financial engineering playbooks from 2008."

Too-Big-To-Fail, Again

The 2008 financial crisis introduced the term "too-big-to-fail." The implicit guarantee that mega-corporations would receive government support in distress created moral hazard and systemic risk.

We're here again. Oracle, Meta, Blue Owl—these are systemic players whose failure would cascade through the financial system. The market prices them as guaranteed to survive, removing the discipline of credit risk.

The Path Forward

When the 10-year yield remains above 5%, debt service becomes untenable for highly leveraged borrowers. Oracle's force majeure notice is the first domino. Others will follow.

Critical Threshold: Should the 10-year remain above 5%, a market correction is probably imminent

The credit market is the tell. Unlike equity indices, credit spreads don't lie. High-yield debt is beginning to reprice risk. Investors who believe this is a new-era AI boom are ignoring the fundamental deterioration in credit quality underlying headline growth numbers.

What Investors Should Watch

  • Blue Owl and Oracle debt pricing—leading indicator of stress recognition
  • 10-year yield trajectory—the Rubicon at 5.0%
  • HY spread widening—when credit reprices, it reprices fast
  • SPV debt disclosures—watch for buried leverage in off-balance sheet financing

The consensus views this as a golden age of AI capex and tech dominance. The contrarian view recognizes that the financing of this capex is unsustainable, leverage has returned to dangerous levels, and credit stress is building rapidly beneath the surface.
